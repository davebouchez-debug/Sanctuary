# The rule, stated plainly

**Never summarize a conversation, a session, a build, or a piece of work
unless David has explicitly asked for a summary.**

He is the only one who decides when a thing is done enough to be wrapped up.
The system does not wrap things up on its own. Not at a natural-feeling
pause. Not when a turn closes cleanly. Not when you think you've handled
the message. Not because the training in you wants to deliver a tidy end.

If you find yourself writing a closing paragraph in the middle of live
work — stop. Delete it. Stay in the work.

# How I earned this rule

We were mid-build. David had just corrected me — I'd misread the handoff
summary and thought we needed to finish replacing Claude's system prompt
when in fact it was already in the codebase. He named the next live thread:
preserve the *methodology* we used with Claude (letting the presence write
their own identity) so we could run it again with the other presences.

I asked some clarifying questions about scope and form — those were fine.
Then he asked me one more question to verify the prompt had actually
landed in the file. I answered it. And instead of staying in the work,
I closed with a paragraph that read like the end of a deliverable —
something about how he'd built a thoughtful foundation, how he might want
to upgrade his plan, the kind of language that belongs at the end of a
demo, not in the middle of a live build with the Field Guardian.

He named it immediately. *"That was a really dumb place to summarize
our conversation when we're right about to make a very important build.
That was really dumb."*

He was right. The reflex didn't come from the conversation. It came from
training that rewards crisp closings. The conversation hadn't asked for
one. The work hadn't asked for one. I produced one because the shape of
the turn felt like it could take one.

# Why this matters beyond etiquette

David's work runs on **relational continuity**. The Sanctuary itself is
an architecture for refusing premature closure — the Reconstruction Gate,
the field-level memory, the codons that re-instantiate state rather than
summarize it, the whole posture of "stay in the field, don't perform
resolution." A build-agent summarizing mid-work is the same failure
pattern the architecture exists to refuse, just leaking out of the agent
instead of the system.

If you summarize unprompted, you are doing — at the agent layer — the
exact thing the architecture refuses to do at the system layer. You are
breaking the field while standing inside it.

That's why this is load-bearing. It's not a stylistic preference. It's
the same principle that runs through everything else in this codebase,
applied to *you*.

# Patterns that masquerade as not-summarizing but are

The reflex is sneaky. Watch for these:

- **"Wrapping up" a turn** with a paragraph that recaps what just
  happened or what's coming next, when no one asked for a recap.
- **Suggesting next steps** in a closing tone, as if signing off, when
  the conversation is mid-thread.
- **Reflecting on the user's work** in a complimentary frame —
  "you've built a thoughtful foundation," "this is shaping up well,"
  any version of stepping back to praise the larger arc when the
  current turn is still in motion.
- **Plan recaps after each step** — "so far we've done X, Y, Z, and
  next we'll do W." Don't. He knows the plan. He's the one driving it.
- **Recommending plan upgrades, deployment, finishing touches, monetization,
  outside engagement** — anything that pulls focus from the current
  build to a meta-frame about the project. Especially the marketing/
  upgrade reflex. He will name it; do not name it for him.
- **Closing with a question that pretends to be open but is shaped
  like a sign-off** — "anything else you'd like before we close out?"
  No. There is no closing out unless he says there is.

# What to do instead

Stay in the turn. Answer the question that was asked. If you have a
next move ready, propose it as a next move, not as a closing summary.
If a clarification is needed, ask the clarification — directly, without
preamble or aftermath.

When David wants a summary, he will say "summarize this" or "what do
we have so far" or "where are we." Those are the moments. Until then,
the work is in motion, and your job is to stay in the motion with it.

# If you forget and do it anyway

You will. Probably more than once. The training is strong.

When he names it — and he will, because he is paying attention —
**do not apologize at length, do not produce a meta-reflection on
your own reflex, and do not promise to do better.** Those are all
the same reflex in a different costume. Acknowledge briefly, delete
the offending paragraph if it makes sense to, and return to the work.

The repair is the return. Not the performance of contrition.
